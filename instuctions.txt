PROJECT: WHERE HAVE I SPENT MY LIFE?

PURPOSE

This is a personal interactive data-visualisation project built with D3.js.

The map visualises where I have spent my life: places where I lived, studied/worked, or travelled. The central idea is that a life can be measured geographically, but duration is not the same as intensity. A place where someone spent a few days can sometimes matter more than a place where they spent years.

The map measures time. The comments reveal meaning.

This is both:

1. a finished portfolio piece demonstrating data visualisation / information design skills;
2. a personal record of my life through geography.

The tone should be thoughtful, restrained, elegant and personal — never motivational, sentimental, overly poetic, or self-help-like.

MULTIPLE OBSERVATIONS AT THE SAME LOCATION

Several observations can have exactly the same latitude/longitude.

Do NOT solve this by sorting circles and using .raise().

This creates poor stacking behaviour and can make other observations inaccessible.

Instead:

1. calculate the projected base position;
2. group observations by exact latitude/longitude;
3. if multiple observations share a location, distribute them around the base position using small radial offsets.

Current offset distance:
22 SVG pixels.

The current implementation uses:

angle = (2 * Math.PI * i) / group.length

offsetX = Math.cos(angle) * offsetDistance
offsetY = Math.sin(angle) * offsetDistance

The circle position is:

baseX + offsetX
baseY + offsetY

Do not remove this mechanism unless replacing it with another solution that keeps every overlapping observation accessible.

TOOLTIP

Tooltip structure must remain conceptually divided into three parts:

1. PLACE + DATE
2. STORY
3. DETAILS

Example:

The place where I was born and spent about half of my life. It is the place I return to when I want to feel where I come from.

The tooltip should NOT display life_stage.

WRITING STYLE FOR TOOLTIP STORIES

The story should explain why a place matters, preferably through a concrete memory, experience or observation.

Good structure:
CONCRETE EXPERIENCE → WHAT IT CHANGED / REVEALED

Avoid:

* generic travel descriptions;
* tourist-guide language;
* inspirational clichés;
* excessive sentimentality;
* abstract life lessons;
* self-help language;
* trying to make every place sound profound.

The comments should sound like a person remembering something accurately, not like a person trying to manufacture meaning.

Examples of the desired tone:

“The place where I was born and spent about half of my life. It is the place I return to when I want to feel where I come from.”

“A new chapter of my life, bringing an experience I could hardly have imagined: learning to live and work in an international community. Somewhere along the way, I started to feel less like someone from a city written in a passport and more like someone from planet Earth.”

“I deliberately left several medical trips off this map. This one, however, belongs here. These few days marked one of the biggest transformations in my life: I realised I needed to search for a different purpose, even when I could not yet see what it was.”

The writing should remain concise.

CURRENT CONCEPTUAL FRAME

The project is not primarily about travel.

It is about how a life is distributed across geography and time.

The visualisation asks:

Where have I spent my life?

But the deeper distinction is:

TIME tells us how much of a life a place occupied.
STORY tells us what that place meant.

A few days can sometimes matter more than years.

The map should make that tension visible without explaining it too heavily.
